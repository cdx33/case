Groundwork

Examining workflows with AI.

I’m developing a prototype to analyse evidence of workflow bottlenecks and suggest improvements. It is inspired by friction I noticed at work, and explores the kind of practical AI and process-improvement work I want to do.

01

The question

Can an AI-assisted workflow audit help turn scattered observations into a useful improvement proposal?

A process can feel inefficient without it being clear where to intervene. I wanted to explore an approach that starts with evidence of a bottleneck, then asks what might change and what assumptions sit behind the recommendation.

I’m exploring this independently, alongside my implementation work. It is a personal prototype, with team adoption and impact still to be evaluated.

02

The current approach

The prototype uses staged prompts to examine workflow evidence and develop suggestions. The stages below describe the approach I’m exploring, rather than a validated end-to-end process.

  1. Collect evidence

    Start from information about how a workflow operates and where friction appears.

  2. Examine bottlenecks

    Use AI assistance to analyse the evidence and identify possible problems.

  3. Review suggestions

    Develop improvement proposals for a person to assess against the actual workflow.

  4. Check assumptions

    Flag missing baselines and unsupported claims behind potential time savings.

Potential savings are estimates to investigate. They do not establish that a change has saved time in practice.

03

My contribution

I define the problem and workflow, iterate prompts and use AI-generated code to build the prototype. My focus is shaping the product behaviour and evaluating whether its suggestions are useful.

The product decision I’m investigating is how to keep an improvement proposal connected to evidence and visible assumptions, so a confident-sounding answer is not mistaken for a proven result.

04

What needs testing

The project is unfinished. I haven’t established that it improves team productivity or produces reliable time-saving estimates. A useful next evaluation would:

  • Use an example workflow with a known baseline and a clear bottleneck.
  • Ask a person familiar with that workflow to check the recommendations against the evidence.
  • Examine what happens when evidence is incomplete or contradicts a proposed improvement.
  • Compare a proposed change with the original process before claiming a saving.

This is the direction I want to develop professionally: understand a real process, build a useful prototype, and evaluate whether the proposed change helps.
